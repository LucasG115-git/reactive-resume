Tailoring a resume from the master profile now happens in three steps: **analyse** the posting into ranked requirements, **select** evidence from the whole profile into a reviewable Resume Selection Plan, and **build** the resume from the reviewed plan. This page records how each step works, the contract between selection and writing, and the limits.

## Audit of the previous workflow

Four parts of the app touch tailoring. Only one builds a resume from Career Knowledge.

| Component                                                                                            | What it does                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Uses the master profile?      |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| `packages/api/src/features/master-profile/assemble/` ("Assemble from profile")                       | Sent the **entire** visible profile (no size budget) and the posting to one AI call that chose 8–18 achievements, reworded them and wrote a summary.                                                            | Yes — the only path that does |

Never reads Career Knowledge.                                                                                                                                         | No                            |

Problems found in "Assemble from profile":

- Every position from the last 10 years was printed whether or not it was relevant, so unrelated recent jobs always appeared and relevance only decided bullets.
- No ranking or budget: a large profile was sent whole; the posting was cut at 15,000 characters.
- Requirements were a flat, unranked list saved when the job was read.
- Selection and wording happened in one call, with nothing to review in between.
- Every visible skill was appended after the picks, producing long skill lists.
- Records switched off or made private in Career Knowledge were still used.

## 1. Job analysis

`selection/analysis.ts`. The default AI connection reads the saved posting into a `JobAnalysis`: title, employer, seniority, summary, responsibilities, domain knowledge, and 8–20 requirements. Each requirement has a `kind` (responsibility, qualification, skill, domain, competency), `necessity` (mandatory/preferred), `origin` (explicit/inferred), `importance` 1–5 judged by the role's purpose rather than keyword frequency, and `terms`: alternative wordings used for matching.

Normalisation is deterministic: duplicates are merged, importance is clamped, and requirements are ranked by importance, then mandatory, then explicit, then the model's order, and numbered `R1…`. If the model returns nothing usable, the requirements extracted when the job was saved are used. The analysed posting text and its SHA-256 are stored, so the analysis is reused until the posting changes (or the user asks to analyse again).

## 2. Evidence retrieval and ranking

`selection/rank.ts`, deterministic.

- **Eligibility.** Hidden profile records, records whose synced fact is switched off or forgotten in Career Knowledge, and records whose fact is private to another application are left out and listed as `unavailable` with the reason.
- **Matching across wording.** Each requirement becomes a term set from its text and `terms`, expanded with the profile's own skill aliases and a short list of synonym groups (`selection/text.ts`, e.g. "donor", "raised" ↔ "fundraising"). Words are lightly stemmed. A record answers a requirement directly (1), through a synonym (0.4–0.85) or only through its entry's context (0.25). Achievements can relate to, but never prove, a credential (degrees, certifications).
- **Score** (0–1) = 0.55 alignment + 0.2 strength + 0.12 recency + 0.08 seniority/scope + 0.05 emphasis.
  - _Alignment_ is led by the single best importance-weighted match, with smaller credit for a second and third, so many incidental matches can't look like one strong one.
  - _Strength_: measured results, action-led wording, reasonable length, always-include; duty-style phrasing ("Responsible for…") counts against.
  - _Recency_ decays with years since the entry ended but never below 0.3 — age alone can't rule a record out.
  - _Scope_ compares the entry title's level with the job's seniority. _Emphasis_ rewards overlap with the user's target roles, priorities and wanted organisations (Preferences).
- **Shortlist.** The whole profile is ranked first; the AI then reviews the best 50 overall, the best four for each requirement, every always-include achievement and each entry's best, within a 36,000-character budget. Every entry is listed with its dates even when none of its achievements is shown, and the AI is told how many weaker achievements weren't sent.

## 3. AI review

`selection/review.ts`. The model rates each shortlisted achievement 0–3 for relevance with the requirement ids it evidences and a short reason, recommends entries in or out, and rates relevant skills. It cites short ids (`E1`, `A3`, `S2`); ids it invents are dropped, and requirement ids are validated. If the answer can't be read, the plan comes from the ranking alone and says so.

## 4. Composing the plan

`selection/compose.ts`, deterministic. An achievement's combined score is half the AI rating and half the ranking, so neither decides alone. Configurable defaults are in `SELECTION_RULES`:

- Recommended achievements need a combined score of 0.45 **and** a real connection to the posting (alignment ≥ 0.35 or an AI rating ≥ 2).
- Positions are decided newest first. A recent position (ended within 10 years) is included when it has strong evidence or the AI recommends it; the current or latest role stays for the timeline. An older position joins only when it shows something the included recent roles don't, or when it is strongly relevant (≥ 0.72) — so a genuinely relevant early role stays eligible.
- Bullets: up to 5 for the lead position, 4 for other recent ones, 3 for older positions and other entries; near-duplicates of an already chosen achievement are skipped; always-include achievements are kept.
- Up to 3 projects and 2 volunteer roles. A thin one (fewer than 3 bullets for a project, 2 for a volunteer role, with no core achievement) is left out unless its best bullet scores at least 0.7 or it evidences a requirement no included position does. The space it frees (its bullets plus one for its heading) goes to more bullets from the positions: up to 2 more for the lead role and 1 for each other, only bullets with alignment of at least 0.5 or an AI relevance of 2 or more, so loosely related work isn't used as padding. The review prompt asks the AI for the same.
- Education is listed by convention; certifications and awards when they relate to the posting (certifications also when recent).
- Up to 15 skills that relate to a requirement, best evidenced first.
- Section order: summary, skills, experience and projects (projects first when they carry the strongest evidence), volunteer, education, certifications, awards.
- Coverage per requirement: **supported** (an included achievement demonstrates it), **partial** (only a weak match, a listed skill or a related credential), or **gap**, with any unselected evidence that could support it.
- Source checks: positions without start dates, the same role recorded twice with different dates, near-identical achievements, and one name used for two skills.

Repeated runs on the same input give identical plans; the AI's role is limited to ratings that are blended and thresholded, so small changes in its ratings move only borderline items.

## 5. Review and persistence

One `resume_selection` row per application (migration `20261102215017_resume_selection`) keeps the analysed posting text, the analysis, the **proposed** plan and the user's **reviewed** plan. Edits change include flags and order only, never profile records; anything that differs from the proposal is marked `userEdited`, and those choices carry over when the user proposes again. The API is `masterProfile.selection.{get, propose, update, reset}`; `get` reports when the posting or the profile changed since the plan was made.

In the app, **Assemble from profile** (and **Save and build resume**) now opens on _Choose what to include_: the ranked requirements with their coverage, then every profile entry in reverse chronological order with what's included, why, and up/down ordering, then skills. _Next: build the resume_ writes from the reviewed plan.

## 6. Building from the plan

`assemble/service.ts` uses the reviewed plan when one exists: its entries, its achievements in its order (uncapped), and its skills only. With _Use my exact wording_, no AI call is made. With _Adjust to the posting_, the AI words only the planned achievements and writes a summary; the existing grounding check still rejects rewrites that add numbers. Without a plan, the previous one-step path runs unchanged.

## Contract for the writing step

The writing phase consumes `ResumeSelectionPlan` and `JobAnalysis` from `@reactive-resume/schema/resume-selection`:

```ts
JobAnalysis {
  title, employer, seniority, summary, responsibilities[], domain[],
  requirements: { id: "R1"…, text, kind, necessity, origin, importance 1-5, terms[] }[]  // ranked
  postingHash, analyzedAt, model
}

ResumeSelectionPlan {
  applicationId, createdAt, source: "ai" | "ranking", positioning,
  sections: ("summary"|"skills"|"experience"|"projects"|"volunteer"|"education"|"certifications"|"awards")[],
  entries: {                      // every eligible profile entry, reverse chronological
    entryId, kind, title, organization, startDate, endDate,   // copied from the source record
    include, reason, bulletTarget, requirementIds[], userEdited,
    achievements: {               // included first, in print order
      achievementId, text, include, requirementIds[], reason, core, userEdited,
      score: { total, alignment, strength, recency, aiRelevance }
    }[]
  }[],
  standalone: PlanAchievement[],  // not attached to an entry; can inform the summary only
  skills: { skillId, name, category, include, requirementIds[], evidenced, reason, userEdited }[],
  coverage: { requirementId, status: "supported"|"partial"|"gap", achievementIds[], skillIds[], entryIds[], available[] }[],
  unavailable: { id, kind, reason }[],
  warnings: string[],
  totals: { entries, achievements, skills, includedEntries, includedAchievements, includedSkills }
}
```

Rules for consumers:

- Write only included entries and included achievements, in plan order; read wording, metrics, variants and dates from the profile records by id (`text` is a display copy).
- Keep `startDate`, `endDate`, titles and organisations exactly as recorded; never fill a `gap` requirement.
- `bulletTarget` is a suggestion; the user's included set wins.
- Treat `positioning` and `reason` as guidance, not as resume text.

## Tests

`selection/selection.test.ts` uses fictional profiles in `selection/fixtures.ts`:

- a communications posting where recent nonprofit campaign and communications work outranks an unrelated early retail job;
- a web development posting where technical projects and integrations are emphasised;
- a project management posting where delivery, coordination, stakeholders and measured scope lead, and a certificate counts only as partial coverage for "PMP or equivalent";
- a highly relevant 2008 role kept beside an unrelated current job, in reverse chronological order;
- conflicting and incomplete records (duplicate roles with different dates, missing dates, near-duplicate achievements, an alias naming two skills) plus hidden, switched-off and private records;
- a profile of 430+ achievements where the relevant ones come last, which are still shortlisted, sent to the AI and selected;
- AI answers citing records that don't exist; identical plans on repeated runs, and only borderline changes when AI ratings shift;
- edits that carry into a new proposal, and requirements reported as gaps.

`selection/service.integration.test.ts` (real PostgreSQL) covers proposing, reusing the analysis, ownership, Knowledge switches, saving edits without changing the profile, resetting, and building a resume that follows the reviewed plan without an AI call.

## Limitations

- Matching is lexical with stemming and a small synonym list; semantic matching beyond that relies on the AI's `terms` and its review of the shortlist. Embeddings were not added.
- Seniority is read from title words; scope (team size, budget) is judged by the AI, not measured.
- Only the master profile is used as evidence. Free-text Career Knowledge facts that aren't from the profile (for example remembered from coaching) are not selectable, because they have no position to sit under.
- The analysis and review run on the user's default AI connection; there is no per-task model choice yet.
- The review interface is on the web; the mobile app doesn't assemble from the profile yet.
- Section order is recommended in the plan; applying it to the template's layout is left to the writing step.

## Profile gaps

`selection/gaps.ts`, `selection/assessment.ts` and `selection/gaps-service.ts`, API `masterProfile.gaps.{get, answer, draft}`, column `resume_selection.gap_answers` (migration `20261107182809_resume_selection_gap_answers`).

- **Fit assessment** (`assessFit`, task `evidence`, step `fit`, prompt version `PROMPT_VERSIONS.fit`): after the selection is proposed, the AI rates every requirement 0–3 on the included evidence, education, listed skills and total years, with a one-line reason and an overall summary: 3 strong and direct, 2 some (limited, occasional or indirect), 1 only adjacent or a listed skill without use, 0 none. The prompt tells it to judge substance, not shared words. Stored as `plan.assessment` (`basis: "ai"`). An answer rating under half the requirements, or an unreadable one, falls back to `keywordAssessment` (`basis: "keywords"`): coverage `supported` → 2, `partial` → 1, `gap` → 0, so keywords alone never reach 3. The keyword coverage used to be the match itself and overstated fit, because any lexical overlap counted as support.
- **Match** (`matchFromRatings`): Σ weight × value / Σ weight, with value 3 → 1, 2 → 0.6, 1 → 0.25, 0 → 0 and weight = importance / 5, × 0.75 when preferred. Recomputed from the stored ratings on every read. The report also returns `basis`, the per-requirement `ratings` with reasons, and the `summary`.
- **Gaps** (`gapReport`): with an AI assessment, a requirement is asked about when it's rated 1 or 0; without one, when nothing in the profile evidences it (coverage `gap` with no `available` evidence). A skill or tool requirement is also asked about when it names skills (`skillCandidates`: its terms and list items, without lead-ins such as "proficiency with" or generic words) that the profile has under no name and no record mentions. Qualifier words ("development", "tools", "systems") are ignored when matching, so "WordPress development" is found in a WordPress achievement. Mandatory first, then importance; at most 15.
- **Skills and tools**: requirement kind `tool` (analysis prompt version 4) marks named software and platforms. Each missing name is returned as `{ name, kind }`; `kindOf` makes it a tool when the requirement is a tool requirement, the name is in the `TOOLS` list, or it has an inner capital ("HubSpot").
- **Answer**: confirmed skills become profile skills with their `kind` and every entry the user chose (`entryIds`); a skill already in the profile gains the new entries. A line (the user's note, or a draft they approved) becomes an achievement under the chosen entry, tagged with the skills. Each answer is recorded as `added` or `no` and not asked again for that posting. When anything was added, the selection is proposed again, keeping the user's earlier choices.
- **Draft** (task `polish`): words the user's note as one achievement. It's checked with `checkGrounded` and `introducedTools` against the note; a draft that adds a number or tool is replaced by the note itself, with a warning.
- Web: `gap-step.tsx` in the tailoring dialog, before **Choose what to include**, with the match bar (basis label, summary and **How this is worked out**) kept above the selection review. Tools and skills are listed separately, each with a **Used at** picker (`career/knowledge/used-at.tsx`) for any number of entries.

## Skills and tools in the profile

`profile_skill.kind` (`skill` | `tool`) and `profile_skill.entry_ids` (migration `20261107195715_profile_skill_kind_and_places`, which marks existing skills named in `TOOLS` as tools). **Career → Knowledge → Profile** shows **Skills** and **Tools & platforms** as separate sections; the editor sets the type and **Where you used it**. Imports mark known tool names as tools (`toolLike`). A skill used at an included entry counts as evidenced (`evidencedSkills`), and the writer puts tools in their own group (`separateTools`, default name "Tools & Platforms").
