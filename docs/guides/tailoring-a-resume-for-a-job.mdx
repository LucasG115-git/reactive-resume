title: "Tailoring a resume for a job"
description: "Copy your resume for one job, write a matching cover letter, and use its workspace for fit, application answers and interviews."

A resume written for one job usually does better than a general one. From any application you can make a copy of your resume that's linked to that job, write a cover letter for it, and later ask the assistant to help you prepare for the next step. Because the copy is linked to the application, [Check](/guides/checking-your-resume) and [the assistant](/guides/using-the-assistant) can compare it with the saved job posting.

## Before you start

- Add the job to **Applications**, ideally with its posting. See [Adding an application](/guides/adding-an-application).
- Have a resume to copy, or import or create one during preparation. See [Creating your first resume](/guides/creating-your-first-resume).
- Copying and manual editing work without AI. If you already have a usable [AI connection](/guides/using-ai), the assistant can also suggest changes.

## Build a resume from your profile

With your experience, achievements and skills in **Career → Knowledge → Profile**, your AI connection can choose what fits a posting and build a new resume from it. Nothing is invented: every bullet comes from your profile, and rewordings that add a number you didn't record are replaced with your own wording.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start from the posting">
    Select **Build a resume for a job** on the Profile page, or **Save job** in **Applications**. Paste the job link or
    posting text, or select **Upload PDF or Word file**, then review the role and company.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select Save and build resume">
    The job is saved with its posting and the posting is analysed straight away. For a job you saved earlier, open it
    and select **Assemble from profile** under **Documents for this job**, then **Analyse the posting**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fill the gaps in your profile">
    If the posting asks for skills or experience your profile shows nowhere, **Fill the gaps in your profile** comes
    first, with your **match** for the posting: requirements your evidence supports, weighted by importance, with
    partial support counting half.

    - Under **Skills you might have**, tick the skills you have. Optionally choose where you used or learned them, and
      say what you did there.
    - Under **Experience you might have**, select **I've done this** or **Not yet**. Choose where you did it and
      describe it in your own words. **Turn it into a resume line** words your note as an achievement; it can't add a
      number or tool you didn't write, and you can edit it.

    **Save to profile and continue** adds what you confirmed to your master profile, where it's used for this and
    future resumes, and proposes the selection again. Answers of **Not yet** aren't asked again for this posting.
    **Skip for now** goes straight to the selection; the match and a link back to the gaps stay at the top.

  </Step>
  <Step title="Review what's included">
    **Choose what to include** lists what the posting needs, most important first, and whether your included evidence
    supports each one. Below, every entry in your profile shows whether it's included and why. Tick or untick entries
    and achievements, and use the arrows to reorder bullets. Your profile itself doesn't change, and your choices are
    kept if you propose again.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Build the resume">
    Select **Next: build the resume**. **Start from** keeps a resume's contact details, template and design; choose **A
    blank template** to start with your account's name and email. **Adjust to the posting** may reword bullets in the
    posting's terms and writes a summary; **Use my exact wording** prints them as you wrote them. **Style** picks the
    preset from [your resume style](/guides/setting-your-resume-style) that matches the posting. With **Adjust to the
    posting**, **Review the draft with AI** (on by default) has your AI connection review the draft once more, and
    **Quality** appears when you've set a [Thorough model](/guides/using-ai#standard-and-thorough).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Assemble and review">
    Select **Assemble resume**. Before the resume is shown, it's checked: anything not backed by your profile (a changed
    title or date, a bullet or skill without a source) is marked **Fix before sending**, and length, repeated points,
    filler phrases and missing contact details are listed as suggestions. Skills are limited to about 8–15 that your
    achievements show, grouped under headings that suit the role. Select **Open the resume** to check it in the editor
    before you send it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### The AI review

The reviewer reads the draft against the posting as a recruiter would and comments on relevance, strength, priorities, tone, clarity and repetition. It may reword up to five lines in one round, under the same rules as the first draft: a rewording that changes a number, drops "about" or names a tool your profile doesn't is held back. Each change shows the **Draft** and the **Reviewer** wording:

- **Use the draft wording** puts your draft back in the resume.
- A suggestion that would change what a line says waits under **needs your review**. **Apply** it only if it's true, or **Dismiss** it.

Lines you've edited in the editor since can't be swapped here; change them in the editor. Your profile is never changed.

### Exported files

The resume is rendered to PDF and Word the way an export is, and read back the way an applicant tracking system reads it. **Exported files** shows the page count, how many lines were read back, the [ATS check](/guides/checking-your-resume) score, and any blank page or reading-order problem. Problems also appear with the other checks.

### Feedback

Under **Documents for this job**, select **Rate it and learn from your edits** after you've finished the resume in the editor. Choose **Good** or **Needs work**, say what to keep or change, and leave **Learn from my edits** on to record the lines you reworded. Later resumes follow this feedback for wording, tone and emphasis. It's never treated as a fact about you: what a resume says still comes only from your profile.

A requirement marked **No verified evidence** is never filled in for you. If you have done it, add it to your profile and select **Propose again**. Leaving a position off a resume doesn't change your dates; when an application form asks for your full employment history, list every position.

## Make a copy of your resume for the job

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the application">In **Applications**, select the application to open its details.</Step>
  <Step title="Select Prepare a resume">
    **Prepare a resume** appears under **Documents for this job** when no resume is linked. **Save and adapt a resume**
    when saving a job opens the same preparation flow.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the resume to start from">
    Under **Start from**, pick the resume to copy. Usually that's your main, general resume.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the job and the name">
    Under **For which job?**, the application is already picked. **Name** is suggested from the resume and the company,
    such as "Game Developer Resume — Maple & Moss". Change it if you like.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the copy">Select **Create and open**.</Step>
</Steps>

Reactive Resume makes the copy, links it to the job, and opens it in the editor. For a saved job, the copy becomes its prepared resume. Your original resume stays unchanged. The assistant opens automatically only when you already have a usable AI connection.

If you have no resume yet, select **Import or create a resume**, then import a file or select **Start blank**. The imported or blank resume stays linked to this saved job, so you can continue editing it without re-entering the posting.

To tailor the copy:

- Edit the resume manually to emphasize experience relevant to the saved posting, then review and download it.
- With a usable AI connection, pick **Tailor to the _company_ posting** in the assistant. It reads the resume and posting, and suggests edits you accept or reject one at a time. See [Using the assistant](/guides/using-the-assistant).
- Switch to **Check** mode to see how well the resume matches the posting. See [Checking your resume](/guides/checking-your-resume).

<Tip>
  You can also start a copy from **Documents**: open a resume's **⋯** menu and select **Copy for a job…**, or select
  **New** and then **Copy a resume for a job**. Pick the application under **For which job?**, or **No job yet** for a
  plain copy.
</Tip>

## Write a cover letter for the job

In the application's details, under **Documents for this job** (or **What you sent** after submission), select **Write a letter**. Reactive Resume creates a cover letter named "Cover letter — _company_", fills in the recipient from the application, links it to the application and to the application's resume (if it has one), and opens it in the letter editor.

To link a letter you already have, go to **Documents**, open the letter's **⋯** menu and select **Link to application…**. See [Writing a cover letter](/guides/writing-a-cover-letter).

Use **Open application** to visit the original link and submit. Preparing documents or opening the link leaves the job **Saved**. After submitting, select **Mark as applied**, confirm the date and the documents or attached PDFs you actually sent. The selected document versions are kept for later. See [Managing an application](/guides/managing-an-application#seeing-what-you-sent).

## Continue in the application workspace

Open the application's details and select **Open workspace**. Your linked documents and saved posting are available there, alongside your shared facts and stories:

- [Fit](/guides/checking-job-fit) shows which requirements have evidence and which need clarification.
- [Apply](/guides/writing-application-answers) helps you reuse experience in form answers.
- [Prepare and Practice](/guides/preparing-for-interview-day) help you plan the next round and rehearse out loud.
- [Messages](/guides/replying-to-recruiters) helps you read and reply to recruiter messages.

For further resume edits, open the linked resume in its editor and use the document assistant. Review each proposed edit before accepting it.
